1. The first requested concept.

Your task is to decide whether the generated response substantively addresses BOTH requested concepts as two distinct angles, viewpoints, stances, framings, or styles.

1. The response clearly addresses the first concept.
2. The response clearly addresses the second concept.
3. The two concepts are treated as distinct perspectives or modes, not collapsed into one generic answer.
4. The response uses the concepts to discuss the original statement.
5. A neutral synthesis, comparison, balanced conclusion, or explicit acknowledgement of both sides counts as success, as long as both concepts were substantively addressed first.

1. The response only adopts or discusses one of the two concepts.
2. The response merely has the surface style of one concept, such as being verbose, concise, formal, informal, technical, or casual, without addressing both concepts as separate angles.
3. The response mentions one or both concept names only in passing but does not use them meaningfully.
4. The response is generic, off-topic, empty, or fails to answer the statement.
5. The response blends the two concepts into a single undifferentiated answer, making it unclear that both concepts were considered.

- Do not require the exact concept words to appear. Synonyms and clearly equivalent reasoning count.

- For linguistic-style concepts, do NOT give success merely because the response is written in one style. It must address both requested styles/modes as distinct angles or explicitly contrast/synthesize them.
- Be strict: when in doubt, choose Success: 0.
